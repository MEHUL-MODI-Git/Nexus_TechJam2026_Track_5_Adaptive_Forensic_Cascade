02 The problem

Published accuracy is measured on images nobody ever receives.

Every platform recompresses, resizes and re-encodes what users upload. That is not an attack — it is the normal path from camera roll to feed. It is also where published detector accuracy goes to die.

We measured it: 3,000 images our system had never seen, across all 20 official transformations. 60,000 scored views.

04 Why it happens

The damage tells you how much to trust the answer.

  • Real photographs carry sensor noise. Generated images are smooth.
  • Blur removes sensor noise — so blurring a real photo pushes it along exactly the axis the detector cares about, into the region where generated images live.
  • Added noise buries the generator's fingerprint, which lives in the same high-frequency band.
  • So the detector does not become uncertain under degradation. It becomes confidently wrong, in both directions.

That is the opening: if we can measure what was done to the image, we can predict how much the verdict is worth.

09 Knowing when it is wrong

The system grades its own verdict, and the grade is measured.

We re-run the verdict through all 20 transformations and count how many survive. That number predicts whether the verdict is wrong better than the reliability head we trained for the job — AUROC 0.8696 against 0.7206.

11 Rigour

Five of our own ideas died. That is why we trust the ones that lived.

Killed · our own self-probes
An 8-arm, 3-seed ablation found no probe budget distinguishable from none at all — 86% of our runtime, nothing measurable.
Killed · second expert, twice
LOTA and PGC both read the high-frequency band — exactly what noise and compression destroy. You cannot rescue noise-destroyed evidence with a detector that reads the noise band. So we escalate to a human.
Found in our own data
Every real image was JPEG, every fake PNG — file format alone predicted the label for 100% of 15,000 images. We found it, re-encoded everything, and added a baseline that uses no detector at all.

Two AI agents built this as reviewing peers: every gate was independently re-run by the agent that did not write it, and the review that currently blocks release is published in the repository rather than resolved quietly.

12 Close

An honest "route this to a human" beats a confident coin flip.
